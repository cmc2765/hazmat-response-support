Overview - How to Use This Tool
Read this information first

Essential Information

  • ARS clinical manifestations are highly variable based on factors such as dose, dose rate, radiation quality, how much of the total body received radiation, and many host factors.
  • Patients should be re-evaluated iteratively as their ARS manifestations change with time after exposure.
  • Clinician decisions about specific treatments and venue referrals should always reflect individual patient factors, and the nature/size of the radiation incident. In large mass casualty incidents, recommendations may need to be modified significantly based on resource availability.
  • See Disclaimers section.
  • Click through each ARS Subsyndrome tab on the left.
  • For each clinical parameter, check "degree of severity": 1 (least) to 4 (most).
  • Click "View Treatments to Consider", based on severity inputs. [Print result.]
  • Click "View Response Category (RC)" to assist with venue referral based on your inputs.
  • Click "Start Over" to clear all previous inputs.

Hematopoietic (H)
Read this information first

  • Changes in blood cell counts will reflect radiation quality, dose, dose rate, host factors, how much of the whole body receives radiation, and when, after radiation exposure ends, the CBC is obtained. Changes in blood count values can result from many other causes too.
  • Lymphocytes, neutrophils, red cells, and platelets respond to radiation with different kinetics, so interpretation of lab values will depend on how long after radiation the lab values are measured. See time/dose relationship of blood count elements, Biodosimetry Tool for estimating dose from lymphocyte kinetics, and other details about the Hematopoietic Subsyndrome.
  • Faster drops in absolute lymphocyte count (ALC) and absolute neutrophil count (ANC) usually reflect higher dose from exposure and more severe ARS. Granulocytosis can be an early finding after significant radiation exposure, thus complicating interpretation of early ANC values.
  • If severity value for lymphocyte and neutrophil counts are widely different (e.g. H4 lymphocyte and H1 neutrophil), repeat the CBC with differential. It is probable that the low lymphocyte count (at least initially) more closely reflects the dose from exposure.
  • Blood loss from the GI tract and/or damage to bone marrow elements can be seen after significant radiation exposure. Seeking the correct etiology of altered blood counts will help determine therapy recommendations.

Gastrointestinal (G)
Read this information first

  • Nausea and/or vomiting can occur soon after brain, upper GI tract, and whole body irradiation. These are non-specific symptoms, however, as many things besides radiation can induce nausea and vomiting. Remember that even severe vomiting (e.g., G 4) can be due to stress or many causes that are not related to radiation. If nausea and vomiting are due to radiation exposure, severity and time to onset of vomiting are correlated with dose. See Biodosimetry Tool for interpreting the significance of time to vomiting.
  • Altered stool consistency, diarrhea, cramps and abdominal pain, can occur after exposure of the GI tract to radiation. Severity and time of onset depend on many factors including dose.
  • Radiation can cause GI mucosal damage and/or damage to the bone marrow elements. Either or both factors can contribute to GI blood loss. Seeking the correct etiology will help determine therapy recommendations.

Cutaneous (C)
Read this information first

  • Severity and time of onset of radiation-induced skin changes are correlated with dose, how much skin area is irradiated, and other factors.
  • Skin changes that occur earliest (hours to weeks) after exposure include erythema, swelling/edema, blistering, and alteration in skin sensitivity and/or itching in irradiated areas.
  • Skin changes that tend to occur somewhat later (days to weeks) after exposure include skin desquamation (wet and dry), hair loss, skin ulceration/necrosis and onycholysis (nail and nail bed changes.
  • See details about Cutaneous Subsyndrome.

Neurovascular (N)
Read this information first

  • Nausea and/or vomiting can occur soon after brain, upper GI tract, and whole body irradiation. These are non-specific symptoms, however, as many things besides radiation can induce nausea and vomiting. Remember that even severe vomiting (e.g., G 4) can be due to stress or many causes that are not related to radiation. If nausea and vomiting are due to radiation exposure, severity and time to onset of vomiting are correlated with dose. See Biodosimetry Tool for interpreting the significance of time to vomiting. Seeking correct etiology will help determine therapy recommendations.
  • Both increase and decrease in blood pressure and body temperature can occur hours after significant exposure to radiation. Changes in these vital signs can reflect many causes including CNS irradiation, clinical infection, and other causes.
  • Fatigue, headache, and anorexia can occur hours to weeks after significant radiation exposure. These are non-specific findings which can result from many causes.
  • Cognitive and neurological deficits can occur hours to weeks after significant exposure to CNS radiation. These are non-specific findings, however, which can result from many causes. Severity and time to onset of deficits due to radiation will reflect both dose from exposure and other clinical factors.

Response Category

The calculated Response Category (RC):

H G C N = RC (Read-only fields)
  • RC is auto-calculated using the highest degree of severity checked for any parameter in each of the 4 subsyndromes of ARS.
    • Example: H4, C2, G3, N1 = RC4
  • In a vary large mass casualty incident with austere conditions, referral decisions may need to change. RC recommendations in this tool do not reflect austere conditions.
  • See Scarce Resources Project publications and interactive tool.

The actions are described in table below, where the response categories (RC1 to RC4) are correlated with:

  • Severity of damage (mild, moderate, severe or serious)
  • Likelihood of autologous recovery.
Therapeutic strategy according to the METREPOL response categories
Response Category (RC) Severity of damage Strategy
RC 1
  • Mild damage
  • Autologous recovery certain
  • Outpatient care or general medical wards.
  • General support of recovery processes.
  • Usually no specific therapy.
RC 2
  • Moderate damage
  • Autologous recovery likely
  • Medical wards with hematological, neurological and dermatological consultation services.
  • Supportive care.
  • Substitutive therapy with blood components.
RC 3
  • Severe damage
  • Autologous recovery possible
  • Hematological-oncological institution/service with reverse isolation; intensive care unit; consultations of all medical specialities.
  • Supportive care.
  • Substitutive therapy with blood components.
  • Stimulation therapy (cytokines/growth factors).
RC 4
  • Serious damage
  • Autologous recovery most unlikely
  • Specialised hospital with experience in all areas of intensive care medicine, particularly allogeneic stem cell transplantation.
  • Supportive care.
  • Substitutive therapy with blood components.
  • Stimulation therapy (cytokines/growth factors).
  • Stem cell transplantation

"Each RC represents a level of damage, which in turn can be associated with a probability of autologous recovery. The requirements for the institution where the patients should be hospitalised are highly dependent on patients' RCs, which in turn require specific therapeutic interventions. The complexity of clinical care required for the patients increases at higher RC."

Source: Adapted from Triage, Monitoring and Treatment of people exposed to ionising radiation following a malevolent act, (PDF - 11 MB) Chapter J, page 266, Table J8 (TMT Handbook Partners, March 2009)

Instructions

  • Select the "drop down clinical severity check box" option for each parameter that best fits your patient's status. Use "zero degree" for any parameter lacking data or clinically absent.

  • Click "View Treatments to Consider" for each subsyndrome based on the severity information entered.

  • Click "View the Response Category (RC)" to assist with venue for referral. RC is auto-calculated using the highest severity grade entered for each subsydrome.

  • Re-evaluate patient at least daily as ARS clinical status changes with time.

  • Important: Click "Clear All" before entering data for a new patient. This will reset/clear data for all 4 subsyndromes.
